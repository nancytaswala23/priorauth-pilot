Clinical Policy Bulletin SC-CPB-002: Cervical Spine MRI
Effective date: 01/01/2026   Next review: 01/01/2027
Scope: Fully insured commercial HMO, PPO, and POS members.

Precertification Requirements
Precertification is required for outpatient cervical spine MRI. Submit the
request before scheduling. Urgent requests for suspected spinal cord
compression may be submitted by phone and are reviewed within 72 hours.

Medical Necessity Criteria
The plan considers cervical spine MRI medically necessary when any one of the
following applies:
1. Neck pain with radiculopathy (pain, numbness, or weakness extending into
   the arm) that persists after at least 6 weeks of conservative treatment,
   and the member is a candidate for surgery or injection.
2. Signs of cervical myelopathy, such as an unsteady gait, loss of hand
   dexterity, or abnormal reflexes. Imaging may proceed without a trial of
   conservative treatment.
3. Suspected infection, malignancy, or inflammatory disease of the spine.
4. Trauma with neurologic findings, or with a fracture concern that plain
   films or CT could not resolve.
5. Pre-operative planning for scheduled cervical spine surgery.

Not Medically Necessary
Cervical spine MRI is not medically necessary for neck pain present for less
than 6 weeks without neurologic findings or red flags, including uncomplicated
whiplash injuries. Repeat imaging within 12 months is not medically necessary
without a change in symptoms or examination.

Experimental, Investigational, or Unproven
Diffusion tensor imaging of the cervical spinal cord and upright cervical MRI
are considered experimental and investigational outside of research settings.

Documentation Requirements
Submit the neurologic examination, symptom duration, any myelopathy findings,
dates and results of conservative treatment, and reports of prior spine
imaging.

Background
Neck pain is common and usually resolves with time and activity modification.
Myelopathy is an exception, because delayed diagnosis of spinal cord
compression can lead to permanent deficits.

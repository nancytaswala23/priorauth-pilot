Clinical Policy Bulletin SC-CPB-006: Total Knee Arthroplasty
Effective date: 01/01/2026   Next review: 01/01/2027
Scope: Fully insured commercial HMO, PPO, and POS members.

Precertification Requirements
Precertification is required for total knee arthroplasty in any setting.
Requests should be submitted at least 14 days before the scheduled surgery
date. The approval also covers the associated inpatient or outpatient stay.

Medical Necessity Criteria
The plan considers total knee arthroplasty medically necessary when the member
has advanced joint disease and all of the following apply:
1. Weight-bearing X-rays show severe joint space narrowing, bone-on-bone
   contact, or comparable findings of advanced arthritis, avascular necrosis,
   or post-traumatic arthritis.
2. Pain and functional limitation interfere with daily activities such as
   walking, climbing stairs, or work.
3. At least 3 months of conservative treatment has failed, including
   medication and physical therapy or a structured exercise program, and
   weight management counseling when body mass index is 35 or higher.
4. No active infection is present in the knee or elsewhere in the body.

Not Medically Necessary
Total knee arthroplasty is not medically necessary when X-rays show only mild
arthritis, when conservative treatment has not been tried, or when an active
infection or uncontrolled medical condition makes surgery unsafe.

Experimental, Investigational, or Unproven
Knee implants without clearance from the U.S. Food and Drug Administration for
this use are considered experimental and investigational.

Documentation Requirements
Submit the X-ray report from the last 12 months, pain and function scores,
conservative treatments with dates and response, body mass index, and for
members with diabetes a hemoglobin A1c result from the last 3 months.

Background
Total knee arthroplasty reliably relieves pain from end-stage arthritis.
Outcomes are better when medical conditions such as diabetes are controlled
before surgery.

Clinical Policy Bulletin SC-CPB-003: Knee MRI
Effective date: 01/01/2026   Next review: 01/01/2027
Scope: Fully insured commercial HMO, PPO, and POS members.

Precertification Requirements
Precertification is required for outpatient MRI of the knee. Weight-bearing
knee X-rays must be completed before the MRI request is submitted, except when
an acute ligament injury is suspected.

Medical Necessity Criteria
The plan considers knee MRI medically necessary when any one of the following
applies:
1. Suspected meniscal tear or ligament injury with mechanical symptoms such as
   locking, catching, or giving way, or with positive examination tests, when
   the result will guide a surgical decision.
2. Persistent knee pain after at least 4 weeks of conservative treatment with
   X-rays that do not explain the symptoms.
3. Suspected osteonecrosis, occult fracture, or osteochondral lesion.
4. Suspected infection or tumor involving the knee.

Not Medically Necessary
Knee MRI is not medically necessary when X-rays already show moderate or
severe osteoarthritis that explains the symptoms, or as a routine step before
planned knee replacement. MRI is also not medically necessary for knee pain
without mechanical symptoms that has not been treated conservatively.

Experimental, Investigational, or Unproven
Quantitative cartilage mapping techniques, including T2 mapping and delayed
gadolinium-enhanced cartilage imaging, are considered experimental and
investigational.

Documentation Requirements
Submit the mechanism of injury if any, examination findings including special
tests, weight-bearing X-ray results from the last 6 months, and the type and
duration of conservative treatment.

Background
In advanced arthritis, X-rays usually provide the information needed for
treatment planning. MRI is most useful when soft tissue injury is suspected or
when X-rays do not account for the member's symptoms.

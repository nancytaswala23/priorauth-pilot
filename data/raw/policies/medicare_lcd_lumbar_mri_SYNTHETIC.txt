Lumbar Spine MRI (CPT 72148: MRI lumbar spine without contrast)

Coverage Indications, Limitations, and/or Medical Necessity
MRI of the lumbar spine is considered reasonable and necessary when the
patient has low back pain with radiculopathy (pain, numbness, or weakness
radiating into the leg) that has persisted despite at least 6 weeks of
documented conservative management, and the results of imaging are expected
to change treatment, such as referral for surgery or epidural injection.

Red Flag Conditions
MRI may be performed without completing 6 weeks of conservative management
when any of the following is suspected: cauda equina syndrome (new bladder or
bowel dysfunction, saddle numbness), progressive or severe neurologic deficit,
spinal infection (fever, IV drug use, immunosuppression), malignancy (history
of cancer, unexplained weight loss), or vertebral fracture after significant
trauma.

Limitations
MRI is not considered reasonable and necessary for uncomplicated acute low
back pain of less than 6 weeks duration without red flag conditions. Routine
screening imaging is not covered. Repeat lumbar MRI within 12 months is not
covered unless there is a documented change in symptoms, new neurologic
findings, or planned surgical intervention.

Documentation Requirements
The medical record must include the treating practitioner's history and
physical examination, the duration and type of symptoms, the neurologic
examination findings, and a description of conservative management attempted
(for example physical therapy, anti-inflammatory medication, activity
modification) with start and end dates. When a red flag condition is the
reason for imaging, the specific red flag must be documented.

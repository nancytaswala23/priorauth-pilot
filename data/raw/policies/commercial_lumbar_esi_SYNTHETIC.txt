Clinical Policy Bulletin SC-CPB-005: Lumbar Epidural Steroid Injection
Effective date: 01/01/2026   Next review: 01/01/2027
Scope: Fully insured commercial HMO, PPO, and POS members.

Precertification Requirements
Precertification is required for each lumbar epidural steroid injection
session, including repeat injections. A single approval covers one session at
the requested spinal level or levels.

Medical Necessity Criteria
The plan considers lumbar epidural steroid injection medically necessary when
all of the following apply:
1. Radicular pain extending into the leg, with imaging or examination findings
   that match the affected nerve root.
2. Pain has lasted at least 4 weeks despite conservative treatment and limits
   daily function.
3. The injection is performed with fluoroscopic or CT guidance.
4. The member is participating in an active treatment program such as
   physical therapy or a home exercise plan.
A repeat injection is medically necessary only when the previous injection
gave at least 50 percent pain relief lasting at least 3 months, up to 4
sessions per spinal region in 12 months.

Not Medically Necessary
Epidural steroid injection is not medically necessary for back pain without
radicular symptoms, when performed without imaging guidance, or as a fixed
series scheduled in advance regardless of the member's response.

Experimental, Investigational, or Unproven
Intradiscal steroid injection and epidural injection of biologic agents for
back pain are considered experimental and investigational.

Documentation Requirements
Submit pain scores before treatment, the spine imaging report, conservative
treatments tried with dates, a functional assessment, and for repeat
injections the percentage and duration of relief from the prior injection.

Background
Epidural steroids can shorten an episode of radicular pain but have not been
shown to help back pain that does not radiate into the leg.

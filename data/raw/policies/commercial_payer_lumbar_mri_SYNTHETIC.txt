Clinical Policy Bulletin SC-CPB-001: Lumbar Spine MRI
Effective date: 01/01/2026   Next review: 01/01/2027
Scope: Fully insured commercial HMO, PPO, and POS members. Does not apply to
Medicare Advantage or Medicaid plans.

Precertification Requirements
Precertification is required for outpatient lumbar spine MRI. The ordering
provider must submit the request before the service is performed. Requests
submitted after the service date are reviewed as retrospective requests and
may be denied for lack of authorization. Imaging performed in the emergency
department or during an inpatient stay does not require separate
precertification.

Medical Necessity Criteria
The plan considers lumbar spine MRI medically necessary when any one of the
following applies:
1. Low back pain with or without radiculopathy that persists after at least
   6 weeks of provider-directed conservative treatment within the past 6
   months (physical therapy, chiropractic care, or prescribed medication), and
   the member is a candidate for surgery or interventional treatment.
2. A red flag condition is present: suspected cauda equina syndrome,
   progressive neurologic deficit, suspected infection, suspected malignancy,
   or suspected fracture.
3. Pre-operative planning for a scheduled lumbar spine surgery.

Not Medically Necessary
The plan considers lumbar spine MRI not medically necessary for acute low back
pain present for less than 6 weeks without red flag conditions, for screening
in members without symptoms, or when the results would not change management.
Repeat MRI within 12 months is not medically necessary unless symptoms or
neurologic findings have changed.

Experimental, Investigational, or Unproven
Upright or positional (weight-bearing) MRI of the lumbar spine is considered
experimental and investigational because its added clinical value over
standard MRI has not been established.

Documentation Requirements
Submit clinical notes from the last 60 days documenting symptom onset and
duration, physical and neurologic exam findings, type and dates of
conservative treatment and the member's response, and any red flag findings.

Background
Most episodes of acute low back pain improve within several weeks without
imaging. Early imaging in uncomplicated cases often shows age-related findings
that do not explain symptoms and can lead to unnecessary procedures.
